# CONSTITUTION
## WISSEN-HAUS EMPOWERMENT FOUNDATION
### A Charitable Incorporated Organisation (Foundation Model)

---

## 1. NAME AND PRINCIPAL OFFICE

1.1 The name of the Charitable Incorporated Organisation is **Wissen-Haus Empowerment Foundation**.

1.2 The principal office of the CIO is to be located at [**UK ADDRESS TO BE CONFIRMED**], and the CIO is established in England.

1.3 The CIO may establish and maintain such other places of work or operations as the charity trustees shall from time to time determine.

---

## 2. CHARITABLE OBJECTS

2.1 The objects of the CIO are:

   (a) **The advancement of education** for young people in Nigeria, Africa, and diaspora communities, particularly through the provision of practical career guidance, skills training, mentorship programmes, and global exposure designed to bridge the gap between academic knowledge and real-world professional readiness;

   (b) **The relief of poverty and promotion of economic independence** for young African and diaspora youth through equipping them with practical career skills, professional networks, and global opportunities that enable employment, entrepreneurship, and sustainable livelihoods;

   (c) **The advancement of community development and social mobility** for young people by fostering networks of professional mentorship, peer support, and international connection that empower sustainable personal and community development;

   (d) **The conduct of research and publication of evidence** on youth economic independence, skills development, employment outcomes, and the future of work across Nigeria, Africa, and diaspora communities, to advance public understanding and inform policy and practice in youth development.

2.2 The CIO is established for exclusively charitable purposes as defined in the Charities Act 2011. No part of the income or assets of the CIO may be applied other than in furtherance of these charitable objects.

---

## 3. POWERS OF THE CIO

3.1 The CIO has power to do all things calculated to further its charitable objects, including in particular:

   (a) To borrow money and charge any property belonging to the CIO as security for any loan;

   (b) To buy, lease, acquire, or maintain any property (whether real or personal) and any rights or privileges necessary for the purposes of the CIO;

   (c) To sell, lease, dispose of, or otherwise deal with all or any of the property belonging to the CIO;

   (d) To employ and remunerate such staff and agents as the charity trustees shall determine, on such terms as they shall determine;

   (e) To invest funds not immediately required for the purposes of the CIO in such manner as the charity trustees shall determine;

   (f) To publish, produce, and distribute research papers, reports, and educational materials;

   (g) To establish and operate online learning platforms and deliver educational courses;

   (h) To organise and host educational events, workshops, seminars, and conferences;

   (i) To enter into partnerships, collaboration agreements, and arrangements with educational institutions, corporations, and other charitable organisations;

   (j) To undertake fundraising activities and to solicit and receive donations, grants, and other forms of financial support;

   (k) To do all such other things as are incidental or conducive to the attainment of the charitable objects.

3.2 The CIO shall not carry on any substantial trade or business other than in furtherance of its charitable objects.

### 4.1 Charity Trustees and Membership

In accordance with the Charitable Incorporated Organisations (General) Regulations 2012, the charity trustees of the CIO are also its members. The CIO has a variable number of members (no maximum), with a minimum of 3 members/trustees.

### 4.3 Eligibility of Charity Trustees

4.3.1 A person is eligible to serve as a charity trustee of the CIO if:

   (a) They are a natural person (an individual, not a company or organization);

   (b) They are at least 16 years of age at the time of appointment;

   (c) They expressly acknowledge acceptance of the office of trustee before, or immediately upon, appointment;

   (d) They are not prohibited from acting as a charity trustee by the Charities Act 2011 (which disqualifies certain categories of persons, including those convicted of financial crimes, undischarged bankrupts, and individuals subject to misconduct orders).

4.3.2 At all times, at least one charity trustee must be 18 years of age or older.

4.3.3 A person may not serve as a charity trustee if:

   (a) They have been convicted of any offence involving dishonesty or deception;

   (b) They are an undischarged bankrupt;

   (c) They are subject to a disqualification order or similar order under the Charities Act 2011;

   (d) They have been removed from the office of trustee of any charity by the Charity Commission.

### 4.4 Trustee Powers and Responsibilities

4.4.1 Charity trustees have the power to manage and control the affairs and funds of the CIO. Subject to the restrictions in this Constitution, the charity trustees may:

   (a) Exercise all the powers of the CIO;

   (b) Delegate the day-to-day management of the CIO to employees or committees;

   (c) Appoint and remove other charity trustees in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution;

   (d) Determine the remuneration and conditions of service of any employees;

   (e) Establish committees and delegate such powers as they think appropriate.

4.4.2 Charity trustees must:

   (a) Act in accordance with the Charities Act 2011 and charity law;

   (b) Act honestly and in good faith in furtherance of the CIO's charitable objects;

   (c) Act with the care and diligence that a prudent person would exercise in managing the affairs of a charity;

   (d) Manage the CIO's finances responsibly, keeping proper records and accounts;

   (e) Declare and manage any conflicts of interest;

   (f) Ensure the CIO operates within its charitable objects;

   (g) Comply with all statutory and regulatory requirements.

## 5. APPOINTMENT OF CHARITY TRUSTEES

### 5.1 Number of Trustees

The CIO shall have a variable number of charity trustees with no maximum limit, provided that the minimum number shall be 3.

### 5.2 Method of Appointment of Charity Trustees

5.2.1 New charity trustees (after the initial trustees) shall be appointed as follows:

   (a) **Appointed Trustees:** Up to 1 trustee may be appointed by resolution of the existing charity trustees at a properly convened meeting of charity trustees, considering the skills, knowledge, and experience needed by the trustee body;

   (b) **Ex Officio Trustee:** The person holding the position of Executive Director (or equivalent leadership role designated by the trustees) shall automatically be a charity trustee, ex officio, for so long as they hold that position;

   (c) **Nominated Trustees:** Up to 2 charity trustees may be nominated and appointed by external partner organisations working in youth development, skills training, or education in Africa or diaspora communities, subject to approval by the existing charity trustees. Such nominees must meet the eligibility criteria in clause 4.3.

5.2.2 All charity trustees appointed under clause 5.2 shall hold office for a term of 3 years from the date of their appointment, unless they cease office earlier in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution.

5.2.3 At all times, the CIO must maintain a minimum of 3 charity trustees.

5.2.4 The appointment of a new charity trustee must be approved by majority resolution of the existing charity trustees at a properly convened meeting or by majority written resolution.

5.2.5 A newly appointed charity trustee must provide written acceptance of the office before, or immediately upon, appointment.

## 6. RETIREMENT AND REMOVAL OF CHARITY TRUSTEES

### 6.1 Retirement of Charity Trustees

A charity trustee shall cease to hold office if:

   (a) They resign by giving written notice to the CIO, which notice shall take effect on the date specified (or if none is specified, immediately upon receipt);

   (b) They are absent from all meetings of the charity trustees for 6 consecutive months, and the remaining charity trustees resolve at a properly convened meeting that they should be removed;

   (c) They die;

   (d) They become mentally incapable of managing and administering their own affairs;

   (e) They are removed by the Charity Commission or by order of the court;

   (f) They are disqualified from acting as a charity trustee under the Charities Act 2011;

   (g) If they hold office as an ex officio trustee, they cease to hold the relevant office.

### 6.2 Removal of Charity Trustees

6.2.1 The charity trustees may, by majority resolution at a properly convened meeting, remove any charity trustee from office, provided that:

   (a) Written notice of at least 14 days has been given to all charity trustees of the intended resolution;

   (b) The trustee in question has been given the opportunity to make written representations to the remaining trustees;

   (c) The removal is in accordance with charity law and the provisions of this Constitution.

6.2.2 Removal requires a 75% majority vote of the charity trustees.

## 7. CHARITY TRUSTEE LIABILITY AND INDEMNITY

### 7.1 Limitation of Liability

The liability of members (charity trustees) in the event of the CIO being wound up is limited as follows: Members have no liability to contribute to the assets of the CIO in the event of its winding up, and accordingly members have no personal responsibility for the debts of the CIO.

### 7.2 Indemnity

The CIO shall indemnify every charity trustee against loss arising from the execution of the duties of that trustee, including:

   (a) Indemnity insurance coverage to protect trustees against personal liability arising from their work as trustees;

   (b) Legal costs and defence costs in connection with actions taken in good faith in the exercise of their duties;

   (c) Other costs and losses incurred in the proper discharge of their duties.

Such indemnity shall not apply if the trustee is guilty of gross negligence, willful misconduct, or breach of duty.

## 8. BENEFITS AND PAYMENTS TO CHARITY TRUSTEES

### 8.1 General Restriction

No charity trustee may receive any remuneration or personal benefit from the CIO otherwise than as permitted by the Charities Act 2011 and the provisions of this Constitution.

### 8.2 Permitted Benefits

8.2.1 **Reimbursement of Expenses:** Charity trustees may be reimbursed for reasonable out-of-pocket expenses (including travel, accommodation, and materials) incurred in the course of their duties as trustees.

8.2.2 **Trustee Indemnity Insurance:** The CIO may purchase and maintain trustee indemnity insurance for the benefit of its charity trustees.

8.2.3 **Supply of Goods and Services:** Charity trustees may supply goods or services to the CIO, or lease property to the CIO, provided that:

   (a) The supply is at arm's length rates (i.e., at fair market value, not at preferential rates);

   (b) The terms are no more favourable to the trustee than those offered to other suppliers or tenants;

   (c) The supply is approved in advance by resolution of the other charity trustees who have no interest in the transaction;

   (d) A full record is kept of the transaction and its terms;

   (e) Such supplies comply with sections 185–188 of the Charities Act 2011.

8.2.4 **Interest on Loans:** Charity trustees may lend money to the CIO and receive interest at a rate not exceeding the Bank of England base rate.

8.2.5 **Participation in Fundraising:** Charity trustees may participate in normal fundraising activities of the CIO (such as attending events, promoting fundraising campaigns) without receiving remuneration for doing so.

8.2.6 **Benefit as Beneficiary:** A charity trustee may be a beneficiary of the CIO's charitable activities, provided that:

   (a) They do so on the same terms as any other beneficiary;

   (b) No more than 50% of charity trustees are beneficiaries at any time;

   (c) The trustee takes no part in decisions relating to their own benefit.

### 8.3 Remuneration of Executive Director

8.3.1 The charity trustee holding the position of Executive Director (or equivalent leadership position) may receive reasonable remuneration for their work in that executive capacity, provided that:

   (a) The remuneration is approved in advance by written resolution of the other charity trustees;

   (b) The level of remuneration is reasonable and proportionate to the role and market rates;

   (c) The arrangement complies with the Charities Act 2011;

   (d) The remuneration reflects the time and duties involved in the executive role, separate from the trustee role.

8.3.2 Any remuneration paid must be:

   (a) Authorised by clause 8.3.1 and by separate trustee resolution;

   (b) Recorded in the minutes of the trustee meeting or resolution;

   (c) Disclosed in the annual accounts and trustees' report.

### 8.4 Prior Approval Required

No charity trustee shall receive any benefit otherwise than as permitted by clauses 8.2 and 8.3 without:

   (a) Prior written approval by resolution of the majority of the other charity trustees;

   (b) Confirmation that the trustee in question has disclosed the nature and extent of their interest;

   (c) The trustee being absent from the meeting and not voting on the decision.

## 9. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

### 9.1 Duty to Disclose

Every charity trustee must disclose the nature and extent of any interest which they have in a proposed transaction or arrangement with the CIO. This duty includes:

   (a) Direct financial interests (financial benefit to the trustee or a connected person);

   (b) Indirect interests (indirect benefit, including through family or business connections);

   (c) Business interests (interest through a business partner, employer, or corporate connection);

   (d) Personal relationships that may influence their judgment.

### 9.2 Consequences of Conflict

9.2.1 A charity trustee who has declared an interest in a proposed transaction:

   (a) Shall not participate in the discussion of the transaction at any meeting;

   (b) Shall not be present during the discussion of the transaction, unless the other trustees specifically invite them to provide information;

   (c) Shall not vote on any resolution concerning the transaction;

   (d) Shall not be counted towards the quorum for any meeting at which the transaction is discussed.

9.2.2 The minutes of any meeting at which a conflict is declared must record:

   (a) The nature and extent of the conflict;

   (b) That the conflicted trustee was absent during discussion;

   (c) That the conflicted trustee did not vote;

   (d) The decision of the non-conflicted trustees regarding the transaction.

### 9.3 Annual Declarations

9.3.1 Every charity trustee shall provide a written conflict of interest declaration annually, or whenever their circumstances change, stating:

   (a) Any financial interests in businesses or organisations that may transact with the CIO;

   (b) Any positions held in connected organisations or charities;

   (c) Any family or personal relationships that may create conflicts;

   (d) Any other potential conflicts they are aware of.

9.3.2 These declarations shall be kept in the register of trustees and reviewed annually.

### 9.4 Undisclosed Conflicts

If a charity trustee has failed to disclose a material conflict of interest and this is discovered, the trustees may:

   (a) Invalidate the transaction;

   (b) Require the trustee to reimburse any benefit they received;

   (c) Remove the trustee from office;

   (d) Take such other action as is necessary to protect the CIO.

## 10. MEETINGS OF CHARITY TRUSTEES

### 10.1 Frequency of Meetings

10.1.1 Charity trustees shall meet at least once every calendar quarter (i.e., at least 4 times per year) to discharge their duties. Additional meetings may be called as necessary.

10.1.2 Charity trustees may hold their first meeting on such date as they shall determine.

### 10.2 Calling of Meetings

10.2.1 Any charity trustee may call a meeting of the charity trustees by giving at least 14 days' written notice to all of the other charity trustees (or such shorter notice as all trustees may agree to in writing).

10.2.2 Notice of a meeting shall:

   (a) State the date, time, and place of the meeting;

   (b) List the business to be discussed;

   (c) Be given by email or other electronic means if the trustee consenting to electronic notice has provided an email address or other electronic contact;

   (d) Be given by post or hand delivery to those who have not consented to electronic notice.

10.2.3 The CIO's Secretary (if appointed) may also call meetings on behalf of the trustees.

### 10.3 Quorum

10.3.1 No business shall be transacted at a meeting of charity trustees unless a quorum is present.

10.3.2 The quorum for meetings of charity trustees is:

   (a) The greater of:
      - 2 charity trustees, OR
      - One-third (rounded up) of the total number of charity trustees

   (b) A charity trustee with a material conflict of interest in a matter shall not count towards the quorum in relation to that matter.

10.3.3 If a meeting is called but a quorum is not present within 15 minutes of the appointed time, the meeting shall be postponed to a date and time agreed by the trustees present.

### 10.4 Chairing Meetings

10.4.1 The charity trustees may appoint one of their number to chair their meetings. The chair shall be elected by majority vote and may be removed at any time.

10.4.2 If no chair has been appointed, or if the appointed chair is unwilling or unable to preside within 10 minutes of the appointed time, the trustees present shall appoint one of their number to chair that meeting.

10.4.3 The chair shall:

   (a) Ensure the meeting is properly conducted in accordance with this Constitution;

   (b) Ensure all trustees have the opportunity to speak;

   (c) Put questions to a vote in case of disagreement;

   (d) In the case of an equality of votes, have a casting vote.

### 10.5 Participation at Meetings

10.5.1 Charity trustees may participate in meetings:

   (a) In person at the principal office or such other location as the trustees shall determine;

   (b) By telephone conference, video conference, or other electronic means which allows all participants to communicate with each other;

   (c) By a combination of methods (hybrid meetings).

10.5.2 For electronic meetings or hybrid meetings to be valid:

   (a) All participants must be able to communicate with each other simultaneously;

   (b) Technology must be reliable and allow all participants to hear and see each other (or at minimum, hear each other);

   (c) The meeting must still be properly chaired and minutes must be taken.

### 10.6 Minutes

10.6.1 The CIO shall keep written minutes of all meetings of charity trustees, including:

   (a) The date and time of the meeting;

   (b) The names of trustees present and absent;

   (c) A summary of business discussed;

   (d) A record of all decisions made;

   (e) A record of any conflicts of interest declared;

   (f) A record of voting results.

10.6.2 Minutes shall be:

   (a) Prepared promptly after the meeting;

   (b) Approved by majority vote at the next meeting;

   (c) Signed by the chair;

   (d) Kept securely by the CIO for at least 6 years.

### 10.7 Voting

10.7.1 Each charity trustee present at a meeting shall have one vote.

10.7.2 Questions arising at a meeting shall be decided by a majority of votes cast by those present and entitled to vote.

10.7.3 In the case of an equality of votes, the chair shall have a casting vote (i.e., a second vote to break the tie).

## 11. DECISIONS WITHOUT A MEETING (WRITTEN RESOLUTIONS)

### 11.1 Making Decisions by Written Resolution

11.1.1 Decisions of the charity trustees may be made by written resolution, without a meeting, provided that:

   (a) A written resolution is circulated to all charity trustees;

   (b) The resolution is approved by a majority of all charity trustees (not just those present);

   (c) Trustees are given at least 28 days to respond;

   (d) The resolution is signed or authenticated by the majority of trustees indicating their agreement.

11.1.2 A written resolution becomes effective once a majority of trustees have signified their agreement.

### 11.2 Process for Written Resolutions

11.2.1 A written resolution shall:

   (a) State clearly the decision to be made;

   (b) Explain the reasons for the decision;

   (c) Set out the date by which responses must be received (at least 28 days from circulation);

   (d) State how trustees should indicate their approval (e.g., by email, signed form, online platform).

11.2.2 Responses may be:

   (a) In writing (email, letter, or online platform);

   (b) Authenticated (e.g., with a digital signature, initials, or password);

   (c) Sent to the CIO's office or designated email address.

11.2.3 The CIO shall keep a record of:

   (a) The written resolution and all supporting materials;

   (b) The date of circulation;

   (c) The trustees' responses and dates of response;

   (d) The final approval and effective date.

### 11.3 Validity

A decision made by written resolution shall be as valid and binding as a decision made at a meeting, provided it complies with this clause.

## 12. MEMBERS

### 12.1 Membership and Charity Trustees

In this CIO:

   (a) The members are the charity trustees;

   (b) There are no separate members other than the charity trustees;

   (c) Membership is not transferable;

   (d) When a person ceases to be a charity trustee, they immediately cease to be a member;

   (e) When a person is appointed as a charity trustee, they become a member.

### 12.2 No Separate Associate Members

The CIO does not have associate members or non-voting members. All members are voting members with full rights to participate in the governance of the CIO.

---

## 13. GENERAL MEETINGS OF MEMBERS

### 13.1 When General Meetings Are Held

13.1.1 A general meeting is a meeting of all members (i.e., all charity trustees) called for the purpose of:

   (a) Making decisions that must be made by all members (see clause 14 below);

   (b) Reporting to members on the work and finances of the CIO.

13.1.2 The charity trustees may designate any meeting of the charity trustees as a general meeting of members. All charity trustees are automatically members and therefore participants in any such general meeting.

### 13.2 Notice of General Meetings

13.2.1 At least 14 days' notice shall be given of any general meeting, provided that:

   (a) Shorter notice may be given if all members agree in writing;

   (b) Notice shall be given in writing (by email or other electronic means if the member consents, or by post/hand delivery otherwise).

13.2.2 Notice of a general meeting shall state:

   (a) The date, time, and place (or means of electronic participation);

   (b) The business to be discussed;

   (c) Any resolutions to be proposed.

### 13.3 Proceedings at General Meetings

13.3.1 General meetings shall be chaired, managed, and decided upon in the same manner as meetings of charity trustees (see clauses 10.4 through 10.7 above).

13.3.2 The quorum for a general meeting is:

   (a) The greater of:
      - 2 members (charity trustees), OR
      - One-third (rounded up) of the total number of members

13.3.3 Questions shall be decided by majority vote of those present and voting.

13.3.4 The chair shall have a casting vote in the case of an equality of votes.

### 13.4 Proxy Voting

13.4.1 Any member (charity trustee) may appoint another member to vote on their behalf at a general meeting by:

   (a) Giving written notice to the CIO (by email or post);

   (b) Stating the name of the member, the name of the proxy, and the meeting reference;

   (c) Signing the proxy notice;

   (d) Delivering the notice to the CIO at least 7 days before the meeting.

13.4.2 A proxy must be another member of the CIO (i.e., another charity trustee).

13.4.3 A proxy may exercise all voting rights on behalf of the member they represent.

### 13.5 Postal and Email Voting

13.5.1 Members may be permitted to vote on resolutions without attending a general meeting, by post or email, provided that:

   (a) The trustees first resolve to permit such voting on a particular resolution;

   (b) At least 21 days' notice is given before the voting deadline;

   (c) A voting form is sent to all members setting out the resolution and clearly stating how to vote;

   (d) Completed voting forms must be returned with the member's name and signature (electronic signature acceptable);

   (e) Votes are counted by at least 2 independent scrutineers (members or external parties with no interest in the outcome);

   (f) The results are disclosed to members after in-person voting has taken place at any general meeting on the same resolution.

13.5.2 Votes received by post or email shall be counted equally with votes cast at the general meeting.

## 14. DECISIONS REQUIRING FULL MEMBER APPROVAL

### 14.1 Membership Decisions

The following decisions may only be made by all members (i.e., at a general meeting or by unanimous written resolution):

   (a) Amending or replacing this Constitution;

   (b) Amalgamating or merging the CIO with another charity;

   (c) Transferring the CIO's undertaking (all or substantially all its assets) to another charity;

   (d) Winding up or dissolving the CIO.

### 14.2 Method of Decision

Such decisions shall be made by:

   (a) A resolution passed at a general meeting by a majority of at least 75% of those present and voting; OR

   (b) A written resolution agreed by at least 75% of all members; OR

   (c) A unanimous written resolution from all members.

### 14.3 Notice

14.3.1 At least 14 days' notice shall be given of any general meeting at which such a decision will be made.

14.3.2 The notice must clearly state that a major decision will be made and the nature of that decision.

---

## 15. AMENDMENT OF CONSTITUTION

### 15.1 Power to Amend

The members may amend or replace this Constitution by:

   (a) Passing a resolution at a general meeting with at least 14 days' notice and requiring a 75% majority vote; OR

   (b) Passing a written resolution agreed by at least 75% of all members.

### 15.2 Restrictions on Amendments

15.2.1 The members must not make an amendment which would have the effect of:

   (a) Changing the charitable objects, unless the new objects are equally charitable or more restrictive in charitable purposes;

   (b) Making the CIO ceases to be a CIO;

   (c) Incorporating a provision which is inconsistent with the Charitable Incorporated Organisations (General) Regulations 2012.

### 15.3 Charity Commission Approval

15.3.1 Any amendment to this Constitution that would result in a benefit to a charity trustee or connected person must be submitted to the Charity Commission and must not take effect until the Commission has consented in writing.

15.3.2 A copy of any amendment to this Constitution must be delivered to the Charity Commission within 15 days of the resolution being passed.

---

## 16. GENERAL PROVISIONS REGARDING MEETINGS AND DECISIONS

### 16.1 Interpretation

In this Constitution:

   (a) A reference to a "meeting" includes meetings held by electronic means (conference call, video conference, online platform);

   (b) A reference to "presence" at a meeting includes participation by electronic means;

   (c) "Written notice" includes notice by email or other electronic means;

   (d) "Signing" and "signature" include electronic signatures and authentication.

### 16.2 Standing Orders

The charity trustees may make standing orders to regulate:

   (a) The calling, notice, and conduct of meetings;

   (b) The appointment of committees and their powers;

   (c) The financial management and expenditure of the CIO;

   (d) The employment of staff;

   (e) Any other matters relating to the management of the CIO.

### 16.3 Suspension of Constitution

The members may, by unanimous written resolution, temporarily suspend any provision of this Constitution (other than the charitable objects) for a period not exceeding 4 weeks, to allow urgent decisions to be made.

## 17. FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

### 17.1 Financial Accounts

17.1.1 The charity trustees must keep proper accounting records which:

   (a) Show correctly all transactions of the CIO;

   (b) Enable the financial position of the CIO to be determined with reasonable accuracy;

   (c) Are kept for at least 6 years.

17.1.2 The charity trustees must prepare annual accounts in accordance with the Charities Act 2011 and deliver them to the Charity Commission within 10 months of the financial year end (or such other period as the Commission allows).

### 17.2 Financial Statements

17.2.1 The annual accounts must include:

   (a) A trustees' annual report;

   (b) Accounting statements showing the financial position;

   (c) Disclosure of any related party transactions;

   (d) Disclosure of trustee remuneration and benefits.

### 17.3 Funds

17.3.1 All funds of the CIO, whether general or restricted, shall be held for charitable purposes and must only be used in accordance with this Constitution and charity law.

17.3.2 The charity trustees may:

   (a) Establish and maintain restricted funds for specific purposes;

   (b) Invest funds not immediately required in accordance with the Charities Act 2011;

   (c) Make grants to other charities to further the objects of the CIO.

## 18. REGISTER OF MEMBERS

### 18.1 Register

18.1.1 The CIO shall keep a register of all members (charity trustees) which shall include:

   (a) Full name of each member;

   (b) Address for service;

   (c) Date of appointment;

   (d) Date of cessation (if applicable);

   (e) Any other information required by the CIO.

18.1.2 The register shall be kept at the principal office of the CIO or at such other secure location as the trustees determine.

18.1.3 Members shall have the right to inspect the register.

## 19. DISSOLUTION

### 19.1 Winding Up

19.1.1 The CIO may only be wound up by a resolution of the members, passed by a majority of at least 75%, made either:

   (a) At a general meeting (with at least 14 days' notice) by a 75% majority vote; OR

   (b) By written resolution agreed by 75% of all members; OR

   (c) By unanimous written resolution of all members.

19.1.2 The decision to wind up must be submitted to the Charity Commission.

### 19.2 Sale of Assets

In the event of winding up, the charity trustees shall:

   (a) Realise all assets of the CIO;

   (b) Pay all debts and liabilities of the CIO;

   (c) Pay costs of winding up;

   (d) Distribute the remaining assets in accordance with clause 19.3.

### 19.3 Distribution of Assets

19.3.1 The remaining assets of the CIO, after all debts have been paid, shall be distributed to charitable organisations which have charitable purposes similar to or in furtherance of the objects of the CIO.

19.3.2 The charity trustees shall, in their discretion, decide to which charitable organisations the assets should be distributed, having regard to:

   (a) Organisations working in youth development, education, and skills training in Nigeria, Africa, or diaspora communities;

   (b) Organisations pursuing objects similar to the CIO's charitable objects;

   (c) Organisations with a proven track record and sound governance.

19.3.3 No part of the assets shall be distributed to any member (charity trustee) of the CIO or any connected person.

### 19.4 Notification

Upon winding up, the CIO shall notify the Charity Commission and provide details of how the assets have been distributed.

## 20. INTERPRETATION

### 20.1 Definitions

In this Constitution:

   (a) **"Charitable Incorporated Organisation" or "CIO"** means this Charitable Incorporated Organisation;

   (b) **"Charity Commission"** means the Charity Commission for England and Wales;

   (c) **"Charities Act"** means the Charities Act 2011 (as amended from time to time);

   (d) **"Charity trustee"** means any person appointed as a trustee of the CIO in accordance with this Constitution;

   (e) **"Connected person"** means:
      - A family member (spouse, civil partner, child, parent, sibling, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew, grandparent, grandchild);
      - A business partner or close associate;
      - A company in which a trustee has a financial interest;
      - A trustee of another charity in which a trustee holds a substantial interest;
      - Any person acting as agent for a trustee;

   (f) **"General Regulations"** means the Charitable Incorporated Organisations (General) Regulations 2012 (SI 2012/3012);

   (g) **"Dissolution Regulations"** means the Charitable Incorporated Organisations (Insolvency and Dissolution) Regulations 2022 (SI 2022/364);

   (h) **"Written resolution"** means a resolution signed or authenticated by members in writing (including electronic signatures and authentication);

   (i) **"Principal office"** means the main registered office of the CIO in England;

   (j) **"Executive Director"** means the person holding the chief executive or equivalent leadership position within the CIO, as appointed by the charity trustees;

   (k) **"Partner organisations"** means educational institutions, youth development organisations, corporate entities, and other charities working on skills development and youth empowerment in Africa or diaspora communities.

### 20.2 Headings

The headings in this Constitution are for convenience only and do not affect its interpretation.

### 20.3 Applicable Law

This Constitution shall be governed by the law of England and Wales. The CIO is subject to the Charities Act 2011 and the General Regulations and Dissolution Regulations.

## 21. GENERAL

### 21.1 Dispute Resolution

Any dispute arising between the trustees, or between trustees and members, regarding:

   (a) The interpretation or application of this Constitution;

   (b) The validity or propriety of decisions made by trustees or members;

   (c) The conduct of trustee or member meetings;

shall, in the first instance, be referred to independent mediation. Both parties must attempt to resolve the dispute through mediation in good faith within 60 days before pursuing litigation.

### 21.2 Regulatory Compliance

The CIO and its charity trustees shall comply with all applicable laws and regulations, including:

   (a) The Charities Act 2011;

   (b) The Charitable Incorporated Organisations (General) Regulations 2012;

   (c) The Charitable Incorporated Organisations (Insolvency and Dissolution) Regulations 2022;

   (d) Tax laws and employment laws;

   (e) Data protection laws (including the Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR);

   (f) Any direction or order from the Charity Commission.

### 21.3 Amendments

This Constitution may only be amended in accordance with clause 15.

### 21.4 Supersession

This Constitution supersedes all previous governing documents of the CIO.

## NEXT STEPS FOR CHARITY COMMISSION SUBMISSION

1. **Obtain Signatures:** Have all three initial trustees sign the constitution above with dates.

2. **Prepare Application:**
   - Complete Charity Commission application form (CC21 or CC22 depending on whether registering as new or existing entity)
   - Attach signed constitution
   - Provide trustee details (names, addresses, dates of birth)
   - Provide proof of identity for trustees
   - Submit CAC Registration Number and TIN (if registering in Nigeria)
   - Confirm principal office address (UK address if registering in England)

3. **Submit to Charity Commission:**
   - Application processing typically takes 40 working days for standard model constitutions
   - Allow longer if any custom provisions or clarifications are needed
   - Charity Commission will issue registration confirmation

4. **Post-Registration:**
   - Establish trustee register system (see Admin Dashboard below)
   - Set up meeting schedule and minutes system
   - Register with Companies House if operating as UK entity
   - Open bank account in CIO's name
   - Register for tax purposes
