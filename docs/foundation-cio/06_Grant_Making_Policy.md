## 1. Purpose
This policy sets out how Wissen Haus Foundation (the "charity") decides who receives grants, scholarships and bursaries, and how it makes sure grants are fair, lawful, well used and in line with its charitable purposes.

The charity's purposes are set out in clause 2 of its constitution: the advancement of education for young people, the relief of poverty and promotion of economic independence, community development and social mobility, and research into youth economic independence.

## 2. When this policy applies
It applies to every grant, scholarship or bursary the charity pays to an individual or an organisation. It does not apply to scholarships or opportunities funded and awarded by partner organisations (for example third-party scholarships listed on the website). The charity only signposts those.

## 3. Principles
- **Charitable purposes only.** Money is awarded only to further the charity's purposes and for public benefit, never for private benefit.
- **Funded first.** No grant is promised or made unless the funding for it has been received or is confirmed in writing.
- **Fair and open.** Opportunities are publicised openly. Decisions are made against published criteria, without discrimination on grounds of sex, race, religion, belief, disability, sexual orientation or background.
- **Independent.** Trustees, staff and volunteers must not decide on grants where they, or someone connected to them, have an interest (clause 9 of the constitution).
- **Safe.** Safeguarding, data protection, fraud prevention and sanctions checks apply to every grant.
- **Accountable.** Decisions, reasons and payments are recorded, and the use of grants is monitored.

## 4. Who can apply
**Individuals:** young people aged [age range, to be confirmed] who live in [Nigeria and any other country the trustees approve] or who are part of the African diaspora, and who meet the criteria set for the particular grant.

**Organisations:** [schools, educational establishments, registered charities or non-profit organisations] working with young people in the same areas, with a governing document, a bank account in the organisation's name, and safeguarding arrangements.

The following **cannot** receive a grant: trustees; the founder; members of staff or their close family; and organisations connected to them, unless approved in advance under clause 8.4 of the constitution, with the interested person taking no part in the decision and the grant being at arm's length and on the same terms as anyone else.

## 5. What the charity funds
Examples, depending on available funds and trustees' decisions: tuition or exam fees; course, certification or training costs; books, devices and data to study; mentoring or career-programme costs; transport to educational events; and small start-up costs for training in a trade, skill or business, where these further the charity's purposes.

## 6. What the charity does not fund
- Anything that does not further its charitable purposes.
- Political activity or party-political campaigning.
- Religious worship or promotion of a faith.
- Debts, fines, or expenses already incurred before an award (except where trustees agree otherwise in writing).
- Cash gifts with no stated educational or economic purpose.
- Activity that would breach law or sanctions.
- Anything that gives private benefit to a trustee, the founder or a connected person.

## 7. How the charity decides who benefits
Applicants are assessed against these criteria, which also apply to the grant-making answers in the Charity Commission application:

| Criterion | What we look for |
|---|---|
| **Financial need** | Evidence that the applicant, or their family, cannot afford the cost without help |
| **Potential and commitment** | Academic, professional or personal promise, and willingness to engage |
| **Fit with the purposes** | The grant clearly advances education, economic independence or community development |
| **Location and eligibility** | Meets age, location and any specific conditions set for that grant |
| **Value and deliverability** | The amount is reasonable, the plan is realistic, and the outcome can be shown |

Where applications exceed funds, priority goes first to those facing the greatest economic disadvantage, then to the strongest overall score, then to the order of application.

## 8. How to apply
1. The charity publishes each opportunity on its website and social channels, stating what is funded, who can apply, the closing date, the amount available and the criteria.
2. Applicants complete the application form (see Appendix A) and supply any supporting evidence, such as proof of enrolment or an invoice from the provider.
3. Applications are acknowledged within [10] working days.

## 9. Assessment and decision
- Every eligible application is scored against section 7 by **a panel of at least two trustees** (or one trustee and one person appointed by the trustees who has no interest).
- Each panel member declares any interest before assessing. A person with an interest takes no part in that decision.
- The panel records its scores and reasons. The panel recommends awards within the budget the trustees have set.
- The trustees (or a trustee committee) approve the recommended awards. **Awards above £[amount] are approved by a full trustee meeting.** The decision and reasons are recorded in the minutes.
- Applicants are told of the decision, with reasons if unsuccessful, within [30] working days of the closing date.

## 10. Checks before payment
- **Identity and eligibility:** verify identity and that the applicant meets the conditions.
- **Under-18s:** the written consent of a parent or guardian is obtained, and the grant is normally paid to the educational provider or a parent or guardian, not to the minor.
- **Organisations:** check the governing document, bank details, who controls the organisation, and its safeguarding arrangements.
- **Fraud and sanctions:** check the recipient and payee against current UK sanctions lists, and take extra care with overseas payments.
- **Connected persons:** confirm the recipient has no connection to a trustee or the founder that has not been declared.

## 11. Payment
- Whenever practical, payment is made **directly to the provider** (school, college, training provider, supplier) against an invoice, or paid against receipts.
- Payments to individuals go to a bank account in the individual's own name, or a named parent or guardian for a minor.
- Larger grants are paid in instalments linked to progress.
- All payments are made from the **charity's own bank account**, never from the account of any other organisation, by two authorised signatories above £[amount], and are recorded.
- Overseas payments follow the charity's due-diligence and anti-fraud steps and use traceable banking channels.

## 12. Conditions and monitoring
- Each award is confirmed by a short written **award letter** stating the amount, the purpose, what it can be used for, how it will be paid, the reporting required, and what happens if it is misused.
- The recipient reports briefly on how the grant was used and the outcome (for example completion, results or progress), and keeps receipts for [12] months.
- Organisations also provide a short activity and financial report.
- The charity may ask for repayment, or stop further instalments, if a grant is not used for its purpose, was obtained through false information, or is not spent as agreed.

## 13. Safeguarding
Grants to or for young people are subject to the charity's Safeguarding Policy (wissenhaus.org/safeguarding). Any safeguarding concern arising during an application or grant is reported to the Designated Safeguarding Lead at once.

## 14. Data protection and confidentiality
Applicants' personal data is used only to assess and administer their application, stored securely, shared only where necessary, and kept no longer than needed in line with data protection law and the charity's Privacy Policy (wissenhaus.org/privacy). Successful applicants agree that their name and the amount may be recorded in the charity's accounts and, with their consent, in published case studies.

## 15. Complaints and appeals
An unsuccessful applicant may ask for the decision to be reviewed in writing within [30] days. A trustee who took no part in the original decision will review it and reply within [30] days. The trustees' decision on review is final.

## 16. Records and reporting
The charity keeps: the published criteria for each round; applications and scores; declarations of interest; minutes approving awards; award letters; payment records; and monitoring reports. The trustees receive a summary of grants made, amounts, and outcomes at least once a year, and grants are disclosed as required in the annual accounts and trustees' report.

## 17. Review
The trustees review this policy at least once a year, and sooner if the law, funders' requirements or the charity's activities change.
